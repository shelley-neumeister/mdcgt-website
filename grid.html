Grid & Regulatory Status

Last updated 2026-09-01 · Sources Apr 2024 – Sep 2026
Core Complaint Pending State Impact Report Delayed Scope: PJM · FERC · MD OPC · State Impact Report

The central dispute: who pays for grid upgrades driven mainly by Northern Virginia's data center growth. Maryland's Office of People's Counsel (OPC) argues Maryland ratepayers are unfairly subsidizing it; PJM and FERC precedent have generally favored regional, reliability-based cost-sharing instead. As of this update, no FERC ruling has been found on the core complaint — but the docket is active, with an amended complaint, a filed response, and a Maryland congressional delegation letter all new since August. Separately, the state's own Data Center Impact Analysis Report has missed its statutory deadline entirely.

New This Update — State Impact ReportConfirmed Delayed

The Data Center Impact Analysis and Report (Senate Bill 116 / House Bill 270) missed its Sept 1, 2026 statutory deadline. Sen. Karen Lewis Young (D-Frederick), the bill's original sponsor, confirms the report is not ready; no new completion date has been set — she is described only as "hopeful."

Backstory: Gov. Moore vetoed the original bill in 2025, citing cost and staffing concerns amid budget uncertainty; the General Assembly overrode the veto to keep the study alive. Young's stated purpose for the report: the only existing analysis so far is from the Maryland Tech Council, which she notes is "not exactly an objective entity" — she wants an independent state look at costs versus benefits while counties continue setting their own rules in the meantime.

Why it matters beyond this page: this is the same report Frederick, Montgomery, and Prince George's each cite as a key watch item. Frederick's application pause is explicitly conditioned on the report's arrival — with no new date set, whether that pause still lifts Dec 31, 2026 on schedule is now an open question the county hasn't answered.

Regulatory TimelineVerified

Apr 2024 ER24-843
PJM allocates ~$5.1B in transmission upgrades tied partly to NoVA data center growth — ~50% to Virginia customers, ~10% to Maryland. OPC objects, seeking to shift more cost to VA. FERC approves PJM's allocation as filed — an unfavorable precedent for OPC's cost-causation argument.
Mar 2026
OPC publishes its transmission cost report (the $7.1B / $5.4B figures above).
May 7, 2026 EL26-63
OPC files a formal complaint at FERC against PJM's cost allocation methodology across the three RTEP windows. This is the core, still-pending proceeding.
May 22, 2026 EL26-63
New this update: OPC files an amended complaint. Subsequent filings (including PJM's Independent Market Monitor's comments, below) respond to this amended version, not the original.
Jun 17, 2026
80 Maryland state lawmakers file a letter with FERC supporting OPC's complaint.
Jun 18, 2026
FERC issues a broader order directed at PJM and five other grid operators, requiring them to defend or revise tariffs, improve cost transparency, and reconsider data-center interconnection and co-location rules. Does not directly resolve OPC's complaint — addresses future costs, not the $2B already assigned.
Jul 1, 2026
Maryland's Utility RELIEF Act takes effect — requires MD transmission-owning utilities to be PJM members and mandates data centers pay for grid upgrades tied to their own interconnection; registers large loads with the PSC.
Jul 2, 2026
Separate FERC complaint filed by three MD agencies (incl. OPC) targeting the 0.5% "RTO adder" that Exelon/FirstEnergy/NextEra subsidiaries earn for PJM membership — arguably unjustified now that membership is mandatory. Seeks refunds on surcharges after 7/1/26.
Jul 20, 2026
PJM's Independent Market Monitor reports data centers drove $6.3B in PJM-wide capacity auction costs — a systemwide impact beyond the transmission-cost dispute.
Jul 27, 2026 EL26-63
New this update — confirmed filed, not just a deadline: PJM's Independent Market Monitor filed comments responding to OPC's amended complaint. Content of those comments not yet reviewed in detail; flagged as a gap below.
Undated, post-Jun 18
New this update: Maryland's congressional delegation (Sen. Van Hollen's office hosts the letter) writes FERC calling the June 18 order "encouraging" but noting it is "forward-looking" and doesn't address costs already assigned. States PJM has advanced $22B in transmission projects across its three most recent planning rounds "needed principally to serve data centers" — a PJM-wide figure, distinct from Maryland's $2B specific allocation. Exact filing date not yet pinned down.
Sep 1, 2026 (this update)
No FERC ruling on the core complaint located in public sources. The docket shows continued activity through late August via eLibrary search, but no final order found. Status: pending, active.

The Other SideAssessment — For Balance

PJM and FERC precedent (2024 concurrences) reflect a "regional benefit" theory: the grid is interconnected, reliability upgrades benefit the whole system even if demand growth concentrates in one state, and unwinding that isn't simple. FERC has previously rejected Maryland's request to shift costs more heavily onto Virginia. Whether the 2026 complaint fares differently is genuinely unresolved — this is not a case where Maryland's position is legally certain to prevail.
