Baltimore CityNOT A PILOT COUNTY

Last updated 2026-09-01 · Tracker run 1 (first full profile)
1-Year Moratorium — Enacted Tied to Delayed State Report

Baltimore City enacted a one-year moratorium on data centers 10MW and larger, paired with a nine-month city-level impact study. Unlike the counties with a single named proposal, this pause traces to concern over a BGE transmission line project at the Baltimore Peninsula, where councilmembers believed a data center would be sited. The moratorium is explicitly timed to run alongside the state's own impact report — the same report now confirmed delayed past its Sept 1 deadline.

Governance TimelineVerified

Mar 23, 2026
Council President Zeke Cohen introduces legislation for a one-year moratorium, defining a data center as a facility with 10MW+ electrical demand, classified as a prohibited zoning use citywide for one year.
May 2026
Bill committee passage, then full Council vote: Council Bill 26-0158 passes with 12 of the body's 15 members as sponsors. Also directs the Department of Planning to conduct a comprehensive nine-month data center impact study.
Jun 16, 2026
Mayor Brandon Scott signs the moratorium into law. Press secretary Jonas Poggi: the pause gives city leaders time to review findings from the state's own impact study before acting further.
Sept 1, 2026 (this update)
The state report this pause is explicitly timed against has missed its own deadline — see the Grid & FERC page for the full cross-county update on the delay. Baltimore City has not yet indicated whether its own moratorium or study timeline will be affected.

Origin & ExemptionsVerified

Verified
The bill's origin traces to an investigation into a BGE transmission line project at the Baltimore Peninsula; councilmembers believed a data center would be built there, raising concern for South Baltimore residents. The Utility RELIEF Act separately halted efforts to bring a data center to that same site.
Verified
The moratorium explicitly does not affect planned upgrades to Johns Hopkins University's existing Maryland Advanced Research Computing Center at its Bayview campus (a $9M Maryland Board of Public Works grant was approved in May 2026) — the facility is expected to remain below the ordinance's 10MW threshold even after expansion.
Notable Provision
The bill reportedly includes incentives for future data centers that commit to zero-emissions energy — a conditional carve-out rather than a blanket prohibition once the moratorium lifts.
