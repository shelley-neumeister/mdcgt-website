Harford CountyNOT A PILOT COUNTY

Last updated 2026-09-01 · Tracker run 1 (first full profile)
Permanent Ban — Enacted Legal Durability Contested

Harford became the first Maryland county to permanently ban data centers, passing Bill 26-011 unanimously (7–0) on June 9, 2026. Unlike every other county on this tracker, the ban is preemptive — no specific project was ever publicly named. What the statewide board's one-line summary misses: the Baltimore Sun's own reporting is headlined "the law might not hold up." Treat this as enacted-but-contested, not settled.

Governance TimelineVerified

Prior to this bill
Data centers were already a non-permitted use under Harford's existing zoning code. This legislation codifies and hardens an existing prohibition, rather than reversing a permissive baseline.
May 6, 2026
County Executive Bob Cassilly formally submits Bill 26-011 — executive-originated, not Council-originated. Explicitly framed as a counter to a competing Council moratorium bill whose discussion included language about eventually creating zoning "guardrails" that could permit data centers in the future. Cassilly's ban preempted that more moderate path.
Jun 9, 2026
County Council votes 7–0 to pass Bill 26-011, after a packed public hearing — 46 speakers, room at capacity, overflow crowds. Council President Patrick Vincenti called it "a no-brainer." Signed into law by Cassilly the next day, June 10 — the first permanent data center ban in Maryland.
Ongoing
Legal durability is contested, not settled. The bill's language has drawn legal threats, and even supporters have warned it may not survive a court challenge. State Sen. JB Jennings (R) publicly flagged the terminology as too broad, warning it could inadvertently restrict infrastructure that isn't actually a data center. Council signaled openness to amending the language later but prioritized passing the ban first.

Capacity & Regional ContextAssessment

Grid
Cassilly's own stated rationale ties directly into the regional grid story tracked on the Grid & FERC page: Maryland imports 60%+ of peak power via PJM, in-state generation capacity has shrunk under the state's own green-energy policy, and neighboring PJM states are growing reluctant to keep exporting power to Maryland. A rare case of a county official engaging the state/regional layer directly rather than treating it as someone else's problem.
County's Own Framing
Grid — scale claim
Cassilly's office states a single hyperscale data center can draw enough electricity to power over 100,000 homes — roughly Harford's entire household count — potentially doubling residential electricity demand county-wide. This is the county's own claim; treat as advocacy framing, not an independently verified engineering estimate.
