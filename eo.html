Executive Order 01.01.2026.16 — Responsible Data Center Development

Published 2026-09-30 · New page, tracker run 1
Signed 9/23/2026 Dashboard Not Yet Live No Enforcement Mechanism Beyond State Incentives

Gov. Wes Moore signed Executive Order 01.01.2026.16 on Sept 23, 2026, creating the state's first uniform review process for large data center proposals. It does not pause, ban, or override any county's own authority — every action it takes runs through what the state itself controls: incentives, advocacy, agency review sequencing, and a new public dashboard. This page covers the order itself. A separate, same-day policy call to repeal Maryland's 2020 data center tax exemption is noted below as a distinct action, not part of the order.

What It CreatesMaryland Data Center Task Force

Verified
Housed in the Governor's Office, with representatives from the Maryland Energy Administration and the Departments of Commerce, Labor, Environment, Natural Resources, Agriculture, and Planning. The Task Force reviews proposed data center projects expected to draw at least 25 megawatts at peak demand and advises the affected local jurisdiction — it does not replace county zoning or permitting authority.

The Core MechanismAligned / Conditionally Aligned / Not Aligned

For each reviewed project, the Task Force issues a written, public determination against the five principles above, placing it in one of three categories. Governor Moore summarized the underlying standard bluntly: "They need to pay their own way, or they need to go away."

ALIGNED

Eligible for state incentives, grants, loans, and financing. Can receive state letters of support and advocacy before bodies like PJM and FERC.

CONDITIONALLY ALIGNED

Same eligibility for incentives and advocacy as Aligned, contingent on stated conditions the developer must still meet.

NOT ALIGNED

No state incentives, grants, loans, financing, letters of support, or advocacy. Agencies defer substantive review until the developer documents all required local land-use approvals.

Verified
Process timeline: the Task Force has 60 days to publish an initial assessment on a public website; developers may request reassessment within 30 days of that publication.
Assessment — Cross-Reference
Read literally, the "defer substantive review until required local approvals are documented" language means a project cannot be found Aligned or Conditionally Aligned while it is currently missing, or has been denied, a required local approval. Frederick County is the first live test of this: County Executive Fitzwater's Sept 14, 2026 rejection of the Catellus/Quantum Maryland DRRA (see the Frederick page) is a local denial that predates this order by nine days. No Task Force determination on any Frederick project has been issued yet — this is our reading of how the order's own text would apply, not a state ruling.

State-Level NDA BanProspective, Not Retroactive

Verified
State executive agencies are barred from entering, renewing, or extending non-disclosure agreements with data center developers, operators, or site-selection agents. Existing NDAs already in place are honored as written — they are not voided — but cannot be renewed or extended once they expire.
Scope Limit
This provision governs state executive agencies only. It does not reach NDAs between developers and county governments, and does not affect any confidentiality agreement a county has separately entered into. Any county-level NDA question is a distinct, county-specific matter this page does not cover.

TransparencyMaryland Data Center Dashboard

Verified
The order directs a public, monthly-updated dashboard tracking: project location, developer identity, parent company, anchor tenants, expected power demand, expected water use, state actions requested, Task Force determinations, developer commitments, and annual reporting.
Not Yet Live
As of this update, no launch date has been announced and the dashboard is not yet published anywhere we could locate. This tracker will link to it directly once it goes live.

Related, But SeparateTax Exemption Repeal — Not Part of This Order

The same day he signed the order, Gov. Moore separately pledged to work with the General Assembly in the January 2027 session to repeal Maryland's 2020 data center sales-and-use tax exemption. That repeal would require legislation — it is not accomplished by this executive order and has no effect unless and until the General Assembly acts. Moore also indicated he would sign a statewide data center moratorium bill if the legislature passed one, which is likewise not part of this order.
