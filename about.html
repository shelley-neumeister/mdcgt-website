About This Project

How MDCGT works — and where its limits are.

MDCGT exists to give Marylanders a single, honest, continuously updated view of data center governance across every county and the state's grid regulators — without telling anyone what to think about it.

Who We Are

A civic information project, not an advocacy campaign

MDCGT compiles public filings, county records, court dockets, and local reporting into a single, structured tracker. We do not take a position on whether data centers should be built in Maryland. Our job is narrower and, we think, more useful: make it possible for a resident, a reporter, a county planner, or a legislator in a different county to see the full state of play in a few minutes, sourced and dated.

Every county in Maryland is handling this differently — bans, multi-year moratoria, application pauses, or no response at all. That variation is itself part of the story, and it's easy to lose track of it when each county's fight gets covered in isolation by local press. This site exists to hold all 24 jurisdictions in view at once.

Methodology

The three-way distinction is the core of this project

Every factual claim on this site is labeled one of three ways. This is not decorative styling — it's the actual credibility mechanism the tracker is built around, and it's the first thing to understand before treating anything here as a source.

VERIFIED

Confirmed by a primary source

Drawn directly from a filing, ordinance, docket, or official record, and cited to it. If we say a moratorium passed 11–0 on a specific date, that's because a county record or news report of the vote says so.

Example: "Council voted 11–0 to pass Bill 19-26 on Jul 28, 2026" — sourced to county government records and press coverage.
ASSESSMENT

Our own analysis

A judgment call we're making by connecting verified facts — a pattern across counties, a risk we think is worth flagging, a read on how strong a legal position is. Always labeled as ours, never presented as established fact.

Example: the cross-county note that Montgomery and Charles draw from the same stretch of the Potomac is our synthesis, not a claim either county's government has made.
UNKNOWN

Not established in current sources

We looked and didn't find a public answer. This is different from "no" — an unknown jobs figure doesn't mean zero jobs, it means we can't yet tell you a number. We'd rather show a gap than guess.

Example: several county pages mark projected job counts "Unknown" because no public source has published a figure yet.
Reading The Site

Filled chips vs. outlined chips

Status chips across the site follow the same logic as the labels above. A filled chip marks confirmed, source-cited activity. An outlined chip marks something weaker — a claim we're less sure of, or an absence we can't fully confirm.

Permanent Ban Moratorium Enacted Active Proposal — all filled, all confirmed
No Activity Found — outlined, always. This never becomes a filled "Confirmed Quiet" chip, because we can't confirm a negative from a search that simply didn't turn anything up.
Scope

What this project is — and isn't

WE DO

  • Track proposals, pauses, bans, and pending legislation by county
  • Follow the PJM/FERC/OPC cost-allocation dispute affecting every ratepayer
  • Flag conflicting figures across sources instead of picking one
  • Update on a rolling cadence and note what changed since the last version
  • Cite every claim back to where it came from

WE DON'T

  • Advocate for or against data center development
  • Offer legal advice or guarantee current docket status
  • Treat a "no activity found" county as confirmed quiet
  • Present developer or advocate figures as independently verified
  • Fill gaps with plausible-sounding estimates

The Fine Print

Not legal advice, always double-check

All data on this site is a preliminary compilation from public sources and is subject to change. Nothing here is legal advice. Docket statuses, hearing dates, and legislative outcomes move fast — verify anything time-sensitive directly with the issuing county, agency, or FERC eLibrary before relying on it for a decision.

If you find something out of date or incorrect, that's expected in a project moving this fast — reach out and we'll fix it on the next update.
